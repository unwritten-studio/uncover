# Goal

## In one sentence

Take Bernd's non-fiction manuscript about unwritten apart into its smallest pieces, and from that card box build a genuinely gripping novel that weaves its story across several narrative layers.

## Why not just edit the manuscript

The manuscript is rich material: people, scenes, conversations, ideas, setbacks, a Frankfurt book fair, a video call with Tim O'Reilly, a bot that says no. But as non-fiction it is told in order, chapter by chapter, topic by topic. Editing it would make it cleaner, not better. The story is in there; the form is not.

So the form is rebuilt from scratch. The manuscript becomes raw material, not a draft.

## The inspiration: *Wackelkontakt*

Wolf Haas' *Wackelkontakt* (Hanser, 2025) interlocks storylines so that each one contains and mirrors the other. What we take from it is not its plot, its characters or its sentences — nothing is copied — but the lesson behind it:

- **Layers instead of sequence.** Several narrative levels that reflect, frame and comment on each other.
- **Architecture is craft.** Suspense comes from how the pieces are arranged, not from adjectives.
- **A voice that carries.** A narrator with attitude, rhythm and timing.
- **Fitting for this material.** A book about books that talk back is the right place for a story that folds back on itself.

## What "done" means

- A novel that someone outside unwritten would read for the story, not as company history.
- Every fact, person and scene traceable back to a card, and every card back to a passage in Bernd's manuscript.
- New versions of the manuscript can still be absorbed without starting over (see [`INGEST.md`](INGEST.md)).

## What this is not

- Not a pastiche or imitation of Wolf Haas.
- Not an edited edition of *Das begehbare Buch 2.0*. That book remains Bernd's.
- Not finished when the text exists: it is finished when it has been read and approved.

## Starting point

Bernd's version dated 2026-10-28, received 2026-10-30. Later versions are ingested as they arrive.
